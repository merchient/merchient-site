The three rule books, in plain words

Meta’s advertising standards
An ad may not say or imply that it knows a viewer’s health, and may not show a before-and-after or a result that is unlikely.
The FTC
A health claim needs sound scientific proof behind it, and a customer’s result in an ad counts as the brand’s own claim.
The FDA
A supplement may say how it supports the body. A line that says it treats, cures or prevents a disease makes it a drug.

What Vera is not: a lawyer. The case file is a risk review, not a lawyer’s sign-off.

Questions

Can I still use customer results and testimonials?

Yes. The FTC treats a customer’s result as your own claim: it needs proof, and if you cannot show it is what customers generally get, the ad has to say what they generally get, close to the story. A line like “results may vary” does not do that job. The audit marks each testimonial that needs the typical-results line.

Are before-and-after images allowed?

Not in ads: Meta’s personal-health rules rule out before-and-after images and images of unlikely results. On your own pages the FTC’s rules apply, so a result you show has to be one customers generally get, or say what they do get. The audit reads the words in your ads and pages, not the images.

What if I disagree with a flag?

Reply to the case file. A person reads it and answers within the business day, with the rule and the reason. If the flag was wrong, the line comes off the list.

Does the price go up if I add ads?

No. The price is set by how many ads are live when you book, and it never goes up after you book.

How is this different from Meta’s own review?

Meta’s review decides whether an ad runs under Meta’s policies, and a rejection names the policy. The FTC and the FDA read your claims on their own terms, on your pages as well as your ads. The audit reads each live ad and page under all three, names the line and the rule, and gives you a safer line. Meta still makes the final call.

Is this a lawyer’s sign-off?

No. It is a risk review: each flagged line, the rule it trips, and how Meta, the FTC and the FDA read it. If you want a lawyer’s opinion on a line, the case file hands yours the lines and the rules in one place.
