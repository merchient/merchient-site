What can you say about rhodiola in your ads and on your site?

The library supports 4 wordings for rhodiola; the strongest is “Used in herbal medicine to help temporarily relieve symptoms of stress, such as mental fatigue and a feeling of weakness” (moderate, 144-680 mg standardized extract a day). Avoid wording that names or implies a disease, a drug or a treatment, such as “Treats depression or anxiety”.

Also called: Rhodiola rosea, Roseroot, Hong jing tian

Reviewed 15 October 2026

Not legal advice. This guide shows the wording that published evidence supports at a daily dose, from the sources linked below; it is not a ruling on any claim. Your own product, its form and dose, and your own evidence still need to match the wording you use.

Supported wordings

What you can say.

Moderate evidenceWording 1

Claims Facts

“Used in herbal medicine to help temporarily relieve symptoms of stress, such as mental fatigue and a feeling of weakness.”

Daily dose144-680 mg standardized extract a day
SupportHealth Canada lists it as a herbal medicine use at 144-680 mg of standardized extract a day; no other listed source covers it.
Dose detailsAdults 18 and older; at most 200 mg per dose; extract standardized to 0.8-3% salidroside and/or 1-6% rosavins. Health Canada also accepts a non-standardized ethanolic dry extract (144-400 mg a day, equal to 216-2,000 mg dried root) and a tincture (1.2-1.8 g dried root a day). Avoid taking it before bedtime.
FormStandardized dry extract of root or root and rhizome
SourceHealth Canada monograph: Rhodiola - Rhodiola rosea

How strong the evidence is

Strong evidence
At least two listed sources support it independently at a comparable dose and form, and none disagrees.
Moderate evidence
One listed source supports it as an evidence-based use at that dose, and no listed source disagrees.
Limited evidence
The only support is traditional use, the sources disagree, or the source itself calls the evidence weak.

Wordings to avoid

What to leave out.

Claims Facts

Ingredient: Rhodiola

Wording to avoidReason
“Treats depression or anxiety”Names mental health disorders. Health Canada asks people taking antidepressants to consult a practitioner first.Disease
“A natural antidepressant”Presents the product as a replacement for a drug class.Drug
“Treats chronic fatigue syndrome”Names a disease; the supported wording covers temporary symptoms of stress such as mental fatigue.Disease

Disease: names or implies a disease. Drug: names a drug or presents the product as one. Treatment: promises to treat, cure or prevent a condition.
