What can you say about zinc in your ads and on your site?

The library supports 5 wordings for zinc; the strongest is “Supports normal immune function” (strong, 0.7-50 mg elemental zinc a day). Avoid wording that names or implies a disease, a drug or a treatment, such as “Shortens colds, or protects against colds”.

Also called: Zn

Reviewed 14 October 2026

Not legal advice. This guide shows the wording that published evidence supports at a daily dose, from the sources linked below; it is not a ruling on any claim. Your own product, its form and dose, and your own evidence still need to match the wording you use.

Supported wordings

What you can say.

Strong evidenceWording 1

Claims Facts

“Supports normal immune function.”

Daily dose0.7-50 mg elemental zinc a day
SupportListed by Health Canada and authorized in the EU after EFSA review; ODS describes zinc’s role in immune function.
Dose detailsHealth Canada: 0.7-50 mg a day for adults 19 and older (0.7-25 mg from picolinate; 0.7-34 mg at age 18). ODS sets the adult upper limit at 40 mg a day. The EU claim applies to products that are at least a ‘source’ of zinc.
Dose warningAbove 40 mg a day of zinc is over the ODS upper limit for adults, and 50 mg or more over weeks can lower copper absorption. Health Canada’s range runs to 50 mg; the claim’s support stands.
FormAny zinc form on Health Canada’s list, counted as elemental zinc
SourcesHealth Canada monograph: Multi-Vitamin/Mineral Supplements
Commission Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, list of permitted health claims
NIH ODS fact sheet: Zinc (health professional)

How strong the evidence is

Strong evidence
At least two listed sources support it independently at a comparable dose and form, and none disagrees.
Moderate evidence
One listed source supports it as an evidence-based use at that dose, and no listed source disagrees.
Limited evidence
The only support is traditional use, the sources disagree, or the source itself calls the evidence weak.

Wordings to avoid

What to leave out.

Claims Facts

Ingredient: Zinc

Wording to avoidReason
“Shortens colds, or protects against colds”Names a disease. ODS reports a 2024 Cochrane review finding zinc may cut cold duration by about 2 days once a cold has started, and makes little or no difference in avoiding colds; ODS also notes zinc is sold in over-the-counter cold remedies, which are drugs.Disease
“Treats acne”Names a skin disease.Disease
“Treats low testosterone or erectile dysfunction”Names medical conditions; the supported wording is maintaining normal testosterone levels.Disease
“Fights viruses, or protects against COVID-19”Names infectious disease.Disease

Disease: names or implies a disease. Drug: names a drug or presents the product as one. Treatment: promises to treat, cure or prevent a condition.
