What can you say about ashwagandha in your ads and on your site?

The library supports 3 wordings for ashwagandha; the strongest is “Used in herbal medicine as an adaptogen: helps the body build resistance to stress and supports energy over time, for example when stress leaves you mentally and physically tired” (moderate, 2.5-6.5 g dried root or whole plant (or extract equivalent) a day). Avoid wording that names or implies a disease, a drug or a treatment, such as “Treats anxiety or depression”.

Also called: Withania somnifera, Withania, Asgandh, Asvagandha, Winter cherry

Reviewed 14 October 2026

Not legal advice. This guide shows the wording that published evidence supports at a daily dose, from the sources linked below; it is not a ruling on any claim. Your own product, its form and dose, and your own evidence still need to match the wording you use.

How strong the evidence is

Strong evidence
At least two listed sources support it independently at a comparable dose and form, and none disagrees.
Moderate evidence
One listed source supports it as an evidence-based use at that dose, and no listed source disagrees.
Limited evidence
The only support is traditional use, the sources disagree, or the source itself calls the evidence weak.

Wordings to avoid

What to leave out.

Claims Facts

Ingredient: Ashwagandha

Wording to avoidReason
“Treats anxiety or depression”Names mental health disorders; the supported wording is about resistance to everyday stress.Disease
“Cures insomnia”Names a sleep disorder and promises a cure.Treatment
“Works like an antidepressant or anti-anxiety drug”Compares the product to a drug class.Drug
“Raises low testosterone, or treats low T”Low testosterone as a diagnosis is a medical condition; no listed source supports a testosterone claim.Disease
“Supports thyroid health in hypothyroidism”Names a disease; ODS flags thyroid effects as a safety concern, not a benefit.Disease

Disease: names or implies a disease. Drug: names a drug or presents the product as one. Treatment: promises to treat, cure or prevent a condition.
