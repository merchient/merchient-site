What can you say about chromium in your ads and on your site?

The library supports 2 wordings for chromium; the strongest is “Helps maintain normal blood glucose levels” (strong, 6-500 µg a day). Avoid wording that names or implies a disease, a drug or a treatment, such as “Lowers blood sugar, or helps manage diabetes”, and keep “Helps maintain normal blood glucose levels” off Meta ads.

Also called: Trivalent chromium, Chromium(III)

Reviewed 15 October 2026

Not legal advice. This guide shows the wording that published evidence supports at a daily dose, from the sources linked below; it is not a ruling on any claim. Your own product, its form and dose, and your own evidence still need to match the wording you use.

How strong the evidence is

Strong evidence
At least two listed sources support it independently at a comparable dose and form, and none disagrees.
Moderate evidence
One listed source supports it as an evidence-based use at that dose, and no listed source disagrees.
Limited evidence
The only support is traditional use, the sources disagree, or the source itself calls the evidence weak.

Wordings to avoid

What to leave out.

Claims Facts

Ingredient: Chromium

Wording to avoidReason
“Lowers blood sugar, or helps manage diabetes”Names a disease. Maintaining normal blood glucose is structure-and-function wording; lowering blood sugar or managing diabetes is not.Disease
“Reverses insulin resistance or prediabetes”Names medical conditions. FDA’s only qualified claim here is for chromium picolinate, with set wording (see fda_health_claims).Disease
“Treats PCOS”Names a disease (polycystic ovary syndrome).Disease
“Helps maintain normal blood glucose levels.” Meta ads onlyBlood sugar is a health-condition claim on Meta, so this wording stays off Meta ads, as potassium’s blood pressure wording does (library decision of 2026-10-15; Meta’s ad policies were not read). It stays supported wording for pages.Disease

Disease: names or implies a disease. Drug: names a drug or presents the product as one. Treatment: promises to treat, cure or prevent a condition. Meta ads only: keep it out of Meta ads; on your own site it is supported wording.
