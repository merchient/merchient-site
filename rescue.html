Free rescue

Has Meta rejected one of your ads or restricted your ad account lately, or has Amazon pulled a listing?

Send it to me: the real reason and the corrected wording, the same day, free.

Send it

What happened?

Don’t delete the ad, set the listing to out of stock or answer the notice yet. Each of those can close a way back.

What you send is used to write your reply and the corrected wording and, only if you agree on the form, anonymised to improve Vera.

Sending opens your email app, addressed to akhil@merchient.com with the subject “Rescue” and your answers filled in. Attach your screenshots there before you send.

Or email akhil@merchient.com with the subject “Rescue” and the same things attached.

Rescues answered: · Approved after the fix:

What it costs, by what happened.

  • An ad was rejected: Your first ad rescue is free: the real reason, a fixed version and an appeal draft. After that, $250 a rescue, and you pay only if the fixed wording is approved.
  • An Amazon listing was pulled: The reason and the corrected listing are free. The Plan of Action, written and followed until the listing is back: $500, and you pay only if it’s reinstated.
  • My ad account was restricted, or my Amazon account got a notice: The reason and the fixes are free. The full account review and the review request, followed until the account is back: $1,000, and you pay only if it comes back.

What that means in practice:

  • Nothing is asked for when you send it: no card, no call.
  • A paid step is invoiced only once Meta or Amazon has approved the fixed wording, reinstated the listing or restored the account.

What Vera doesn’t fix.

Payment or identity problems, hacked accounts, counterfeit or intellectual-property complaints, and anything that isn’t about what the ad or listing says. No charge for those. When the cause is one of these, we say so the same day and point you to the page Meta or Amazon has for it.

Not about claims? The free account checklist

What happens next.

  1. You get a note straight away that it arrived.
  2. The same day, you get the real reason, quoting the rule it broke, and the corrected wording that keeps your hook. For an ad, an appeal draft too.
  3. You resubmit, appeal or send the Plan of Action or review request yourself. You stay in charge of your accounts: nobody else touches your ads or listings.
  4. Tell us the outcome with one reply: approved, or still rejected. If it is still rejected, send the new notice and we look again the same day.

If it’s approved, we’ll ask for one sentence we may quote, if you’re happy to. If your rescue was free, the quote says so next to it.

Private.

Never send passwords or logins; we never need them. We do not sell what you send, and we do not show your ad or name your brand anywhere without your permission. It is kept for 180 days after the rescue closes, and never more than 365 days after it arrived. To have it deleted at once, email akhil@merchient.com with the subject “Delete my rescue”. Our privacy policy lists the providers that process it.

Small print: Risk review, not a lawyer’s opinion. No promise that Meta or Amazon will approve an ad, reinstate a listing or restore an account: they decide, and they act for reasons beyond claims.

Not about claims? The free account checklist.

Meta stops ads for reasons that have nothing to do with the words. Check these first. Each line quotes Meta’s own page, as Merchient’s library read it on 19 October 2026; Meta changes its pages often, so follow the link.

  1. Payments. “When the payment method you’re using to run ads on Facebook, Messenger, Instagram or Meta Audience Network fails, your ads are paused and your ad account is disabled until you pay the amount due.” Fix a failed payment issue on Meta
  2. A daily spending limit. “When you reach your limit, your ads pause and resume the next day when the limit resets.” About daily spending limits set by Meta
  3. Unusual activity. Meta lists, among its reasons to restrict advertising: “Meta notices unusual payment or account activity, high rates of failed payments or disputed payments.” About advertising restrictions
  4. Two-factor authentication for every admin. “If two-factor authentication isn’t turned on, Meta may restrict or remove people’s access to advertising, remove their permission to manage or edit their shop, or remove a payment method.” About two-factor authentication for your business portfolio
  5. A hacked account. Admins you don’t know, changed emails, ads you did not create. How do I secure a hacked account?
  6. The landing page. “The products and services promoted in an ad must match those promoted on the landing page.” Introduction to the Advertising Standards
  7. An ad that ran, then was rejected. “Previously approved ads can be selected for another review for various reasons, including if people hide, block, report or otherwise provide negative feedback about an ad.” Why some ads are approved, then rejected
  8. The whole ad account restricted, not one ad. That is a different review: “There is a limited number of times you can request an advertising restriction review.” Request a review for a restricted advertising account
  9. Before you delete a rejected ad. “If you delete the ad, you may lose your ability to request another review if you believe your ad was incorrectly rejected.” How to troubleshoot a rejected ad
  10. Tracking data. “If you send events to Meta from a website domain that violates Meta’s terms and policies, the domain may be blocked from sending events to Meta.” That affects measurement, not the ad’s approval, and a rescue cannot lift it. Troubleshoot blocked website domains
  11. On Amazon, the notice is in Account Health. Read it before you change the listing.
