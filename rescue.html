Free rescue

Meta rejected your ad? Amazon pulled a listing? Send it.

We’ll tell you the real reason and send back a fixed version and an appeal draft the same day. Free for up to 3 ads in your first month.

Send it

Sending opens your email app, addressed to akhil@merchient.com with the subject “Rescue” and your answers filled in. Attach your screenshots there before you send.

Or email the ad, the notice and your screenshots to akhil@merchient.com with the subject “Rescue”.

What happens after you send it.

  1. Your form goes to Merchient’s rescue intake at api.merchient.com, screenshots included. Nothing is posted to Meta or Amazon, and nothing is sent to anyone else. If that service does not answer, your email app opens with your answers filled in, and you send the email yourself.
  2. Akhil reads it the same day. Vera checks the words against the written rules (the FDA’s, the FTC’s, Meta’s and Amazon’s) and the notice against what each platform actually acts on. A person reads every rescue before anything goes back.
  3. You get one email back, the same day on US business days: the real reason in plain words, with the rule quoted; a fixed version that keeps the hook; and a draft of your appeal in the platform’s own terms.
  4. You decide, and you submit. We never act in your accounts. You paste the fixed ad or listing and send the appeal yourself; Meta or Amazon makes the final call, and we make no promise about it.
  5. If the notice isn’t about claims, we say so in that email and point you to the free account checklist below. Payments, tools, account security and Meta’s limits on tracking data are outside what a rescue can fix.

Not about claims? The free account checklist.

Meta stops ads for reasons that have nothing to do with the words. Check these first. Each line quotes Meta’s own page, as Merchient’s library read it on 19 October 2026; Meta changes its pages often, so follow the link.

  1. Payments. “When the payment method you’re using to run ads on Facebook, Messenger, Instagram or Meta Audience Network fails, your ads are paused and your ad account is disabled until you pay the amount due.” Fix a failed payment issue on Meta
  2. A daily spending limit. “When you reach your limit, your ads pause and resume the next day when the limit resets.” About daily spending limits set by Meta
  3. Unusual activity. Meta lists, among its reasons to restrict advertising: “Meta notices unusual payment or account activity, high rates of failed payments or disputed payments.” About advertising restrictions
  4. Two-factor authentication for every admin. “If two-factor authentication isn’t turned on, Meta may restrict or remove people’s access to advertising, remove their permission to manage or edit their shop, or remove a payment method.” About two-factor authentication for your business portfolio
  5. A hacked account. Admins you don’t know, changed emails, ads you did not create. How do I secure a hacked account?
  6. The landing page. “The products and services promoted in an ad must match those promoted on the landing page.” Introduction to the Advertising Standards
  7. An ad that ran, then was rejected. “Previously approved ads can be selected for another review for various reasons, including if people hide, block, report or otherwise provide negative feedback about an ad.” Why some ads are approved, then rejected
  8. The whole ad account restricted, not one ad. That is a different review: “There is a limited number of times you can request an advertising restriction review.” Request a review for a restricted advertising account
  9. Before you delete a rejected ad. “If you delete the ad, you may lose your ability to request another review if you believe your ad was incorrectly rejected.” How to troubleshoot a rejected ad
  10. Tracking data. “If you send events to Meta from a website domain that violates Meta’s terms and policies, the domain may be blocked from sending events to Meta.” That affects measurement, not the ad’s approval, and a rescue cannot lift it. Troubleshoot blocked website domains
  11. On Amazon, the notice is in Account Health. Read it before you change the listing.
